On <Zh>生日快乐</Zh> and the handful of phrases that make it sound like you mean it.

## Six phrases for the next birthday you're at

<Zh>生日快乐</Zh> is correct, safe, and understood everywhere. But it's also what every textbook learner reaches for. Here are six phrases that'll actually make you sound like you know what you're saying.

## Phrase one · The basic

<LanguageExample lang="chinese" phrase={"生日快乐!"} transliteration={"shēngrì kuàilè!"} gloss={"Happy birthday! — the universal, safe-for-anyone version"} />

The universal, safe-for-anyone version. <Zh>生日</Zh> (shēngrì) means "birthday," <Zh>快乐</Zh> (kuàilè) means "happy." Works in any setting, formal or casual.

## Phrase two · The complete sentence

<LanguageExample lang="chinese" phrase={"祝你生日快乐!"} transliteration={"zhù nǐ shēngrì kuàilè!"} gloss={"I wish you a happy birthday! — the actual verb-based sentence"} />

<Zh>祝</Zh> (zhù) means "to wish" — this is the actual verb-based sentence version, not just the short phrase. Slightly warmer and more complete than Phrase One. This is also the line inside the Chinese "Happy Birthday" song itself.

## Phrase three · For elders & milestones

<LanguageExample lang="chinese" phrase={"长命百岁!"} transliteration={"chángmìng bǎisuì!"} gloss={"May you live to be a hundred!"} />

A traditional longevity wish, reserved for older relatives or milestone birthdays — 60th, 70th, 80th. In Chinese tradition, the milestone birthdays are the ones that actually matter.

## Phrase four · The wish-maker

<LanguageExample lang="chinese" phrase={"心想事成!"} transliteration={"xīnxiǎng shìchéng!"} gloss={"May your wishes come true!"} />

Said right after someone blows out the candles — the Chinese equivalent of "I hope your wish comes true." Literally: "heart thinks, thing becomes."

## Phrase five · The all-purpose blessing

<LanguageExample lang="chinese" phrase={"万事如意!"} transliteration={"wànshì rúyì!"} gloss={"May everything go your way!"} />

Not birthday-specific — this is a general good-fortune wish used at New Year, weddings, and birthdays alike. Pairing it after <Zh>生日快乐</Zh> is common and sounds noticeably more fluent than the phrase alone.

## Phrase six · The playful compliment

<LanguageExample lang="chinese" phrase={"越来越漂亮/帅了!"} transliteration={"yuè lái yuè piàoliang / shuài le!"} gloss={"You get more beautiful / handsome every year!"} />

A common, affectionate birthday compliment — <Zh>漂亮</Zh> (piàoliang) for a woman, <Zh>帅</Zh> (shuài) for a man. The <Zh>越来越</Zh> (yuè lái yuè) structure means "more and more" — a genuinely useful pattern beyond just birthdays.

<PullQuote>Six phrases, and you've gone from \"generic textbook greeting\" to someone who actually sounds like they understand the occasion — the difference between reciting a phrase and knowing what it's doing.</PullQuote>
